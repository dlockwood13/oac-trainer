The two traps this app is built around

Register. A directive, compliance-first tone reads as enforcement. The mark scheme rewards compassion, affirmation and rapport with risk held quietly in view. The role play module trains the switch deliberately.

Altitude. Programme-level, consultant-voice examples score worse than practitioner-level ones. Every interview drill here prompts for first person singular: what you personally did with a person, not what you led.

The six behaviours

These are the whole mark scheme. Learn them well enough to recognise which behaviour a task is really testing, then evidence it deliberately. Each card gives you what assessors look for, what weak answers do, and where your own background helps or hurts. Test yourself afterwards in the recall deck.

Written skills (marked separately)

Clear structure, readable prose, grammar and spelling mostly correct. The official guidance is blunt: if your answer cannot be read, it cannot be marked. Type your drills in this app under time pressure so your error rate at speed is a known quantity before the day.

Written test

75 minutes on the day: analyse the material presented, respond with brief written summaries. No probation knowledge is expected and none is marked, so never bluff procedure. State principles instead: safety first, verify before concluding, record, escalate to your senior probation officer, keep commitments. Four behaviours plus written skills are scored here.

Method: SCAN, DECIDE, WRITE

Scan (first fifth of the time): read the task question first, then the materials, marking risk, deadlines and people. Decide (one minute): choose your line and your structure before typing. Write (the rest): short paragraphs or clear headed sections, reasoning stated for every decision, two minutes reserved at the end to fix errors. Brief and complete beats long and unfinished.

Full simulation

All four tasks back to back against a single 75 minute clock, matching the real format's demand on stamina and time discipline. Do this at least twice before the day, once in the same time slot as your real session.

Role play

Ten minutes as a trainee probation officer with a person on probation. Assessors want natural responses, not performance, and no job knowledge. What is marked is how you interact: rapport, honesty, judgement and momentum. Work through the decision drills first, then run scenarios free-form against the phased timer, speaking your side aloud.

The ten minute arc

Minutes 0 to 2, connect: greet, open question, listen, reflect back. 2 to 6, explore: follow their agenda, affirm genuine positives, gather the facts you need. 6 to 8, hold the line: the scenario will push you; be honest about what you must do, without threat. 8 to 10, close: a clear decision you can justify, concrete next steps, and a reason to keep engaging.

Micro-skills to lean on (OARS): Open questions, Affirmations, Reflective listening, Summaries. You know these from motivational interviewing; the role play is where they earn marks.

Register switch drill

Read the enforcement-register line, say your rewrite aloud, then reveal a probation-register version. The gap between your instinct and the reveal is the training signal.

Blended interview

42 minutes, mixing behaviour questions about your past with strengths questions about what motivates and energises you. Personal and professional examples are both welcome. Behaviour answers can and should be prepared; strengths answers cannot, by design, so the drill for those is familiarity with the styles and answering fast with detail and energy.

Answer shape for behaviour questions

One line of situation, then the bulk on what you personally did, then the result and what it changed. First person singular throughout: I sat with, I asked, I decided. Two rules that never bend: keep every example practitioner-level rather than programme-level, and never reference disputes or negative circumstances around leaving any employer.

Behaviour question bank

Six questions per interview behaviour. Pick one, take the three minute timer, answer aloud, then rate yourself honestly.

Strengths familiarisation

The real assessed strengths are not disclosed and rehearsed answers are exactly what this format is built to see through. These questions copy the four published styles so the format holds no surprises: leading questions mean what they say, there are no hidden meanings, and interviewers may repeat a question. Sixty seconds of thinking is too long; answer as yourself, with detail.
